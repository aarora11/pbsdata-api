How we check this data

A PBS medicine is described across more than 30 separate government data tables. Joining them correctly is where most errors happen, so we check our joined result against independent sources, every month.

Against pbs.gov.au itself

In August 2026 we compared 100 items, across 15 PBS programs and all four access types, field by field with their pages on pbs.gov.au. 658 of 659 checks matched (99.8%): medicine name, brands, manufacturer, quantity, repeats, patient charge and listing type. The one difference was a naming choice we have since changed.

You can repeat that check on any item: every item on this site has a Check on PBS link to its official page.

Against PBS's own joined data, on every update

PBS publishes one pre-joined view of each item. Every month, before anything is published, we compare our drug classes, restrictions, prescribing notes, dispensing rules, prescriber types and manufacturers against it. If any of them disagree, the month is rejected and the previous month stays online.

What we leave out

Anything we can't check against PBS isn't shown. 60-day prescriptions are shown because they can be checked: in August 2026 we checked every 60-day item code against PBS's own published 60-day list, and all 806 matched. PBS doesn't publish which 30-day listing each one replaces, so we pair them ourselves and show no saving where a pairing isn't certain.

What we calculate

PBS doesn't publish a concession-card price per brand. We calculate it with PBS's own patient-charge formula, which reproduces PBS's published general charge on over 99% of items. Everything else is shown as PBS publishes it.
