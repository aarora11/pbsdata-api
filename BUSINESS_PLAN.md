# PBSdata.io — Business Plan

*Revised 2026-08-12. Supersedes the April 2026 draft, which was written before the
migration deadline passed and materially overstated the opportunity. All figures AUD,
ex-GST. Revenue model is generated by `scripts/revenue_model.py` — change the assumptions
and re-run rather than trusting the tables below.*

---

## 1. The honest one-liner

PBS data is free. Getting it *right* is not. We sell a correct, current, queryable PBS
schedule to the ~84 Australian organisations who need one and would rather not maintain it
themselves.

This is not a venture-scale business. It is a plausible $150K–$500K/year one-person
software business with an option on something larger. The plan below is written to that
target, because writing it to a larger one is how the previous draft went wrong.

---

## 2. What changed since the last draft

The April plan was built on a forcing function: PBS XML and Text files were discontinued
1 May 2026, and every vendor using them would have to migrate. That was true. It is also
**over**. It is now August 2026. The vendors migrated in April, without us, and most built
their own pipelines. We did not ship into the wave; we watched it go past.

Three further corrections:

| Old claim | Reality |
|---|---|
| "The government API is free but raw" | The government also publishes **free monthly CSVs of every API table**. The real competitor is `cron + a loader script`, not MIMS. |
| "80 paying self-serve customers in Year 1" | There is no self-serve long tail in a market of ~84 reachable accounts. Every customer is a named account and a conversation. |
| "$234K ARR in Year 1" | ~$227K ARR is a realistic **Year 3** base case. The old Year 1 number was off by roughly two years. |

**What survived the review, and is stronger than the old draft claimed:** the structural
gap is real and permanent. See §3.

## 3. Why this is a business at all

### The government API cannot serve applications

The public PBS API is **rate-limited to one request per twenty seconds, shared amongst all
concurrent users** — roughly 4,300 requests per day for the entire country. The Department
states plainly that it "has not been designed as a high-volume real-time system, but rather
as a data storage and retrieval system."

No one can build a query-time product on it. Ever. That is not a rough edge the government
will smooth out; it is the stated design. Anyone who wants PBS data at runtime must host a
copy. The only question is whether they build that copy or rent it.

### But the free CSVs mean "access" is not the product

A competent team can download the monthly CSVs and load them into Postgres in a week. If we
sell "access to PBS data," we lose to a cron job. We are not selling access.

### We are selling correctness

The joins are genuinely treacherous, and we have the receipts. Our own July 2026 audit —
against PBS's own pre-joined `/item-overview` endpoint, an independent source — found that
essentially every defect was **a mapping invented rather than read from the spec**, each
hidden behind tests that asserted the code's own assumptions:

- item grain was keyed wrongly and had to be re-keyed
- `item_dispensing_rules` stored the wrong identifier, silently discarding 7 of 17
  dispensing rules
- `primary_manufacturer` was always null
- restrictions, notes and cautions were conflated, dropping three fields entirely

Every vendor who built a pipeline in a hurry in April has some subset of these bugs **in
production right now, and does not know it**. In a domain where the output is what a patient
pays and what a pharmacist is allowed to dispense, that is not an academic concern.

That is the pitch, and it has a property the migration-deadline pitch did not: **it gets
stronger with time.** Self-built pipelines drift, the engineer who wrote it leaves, and the
schedule changes underneath it every month.

### What we can prove that they cannot

- 8 of 8 Tier A joins validated against PBS's own answer, **enforced on every ingest** — a
  bad join marks the schedule `failed` rather than serving wrong data
- Raw landing layer with 0 diffs across ~10.1M fields, also enforced
- Change feed validated at 0 false positives / 0 false negatives on 2026-06
- 100 items spot-checked against pbs.gov.au at 99.8% pass
- 341 automated tests, including a regression suite pinning every audit finding

This is the asset. Not the 99 endpoints.

## 4. Current state — verified, not aspirational

**Built and working:**

- 99 endpoints, 33 routers, ~12.8k lines api+ingest, 42 tables, 341 tests passing
- Live data: 2026-05, 2026-06, 2026-08 all `complete` — 44,483 items, 770,150 raw feed rows
- Automated monthly ingest (APScheduler), quality gates, Slack failure alerting
- Auth, per-key rate limiting, five-tier gating, HMAC-signed webhooks
- Docker + Railway config, auto-migrating start

**Not built, in priority order:**

1. **`pbsdata.io` is not registered.** The brand in every document we own is unowned.
2. **Data licensing is unresolved.** See §9 — this is a go/no-go, not a task.
3. Nothing is deployed. `api.pbsdata.io` does not resolve.
4. Stripe: two empty config strings, zero integration.
5. Tier B/C derivations (patient cost, safety net, substitution, all `/market/*`
   aggregates) are **entirely unvalidated**. These are exactly what §7's Line B sells.
6. No pricing, no public docs, no status page, no SLA, no contracts.

Note the ordering. Items 1 and 2 cost about $100 and two emails. Item 4 — the one the old
plan called "the blocker to revenue" — is fifth, and is not a blocker at all. You can invoice
your first three customers manually.

## 5. Who actually buys

Counted bottom-up by naming the buyers. "Reachable" means a solo operator can plausibly get
a meeting *and* they can plausibly buy from a one-person company — which rules out most
hospital and health-fund procurement.

The first three rows are **enumerated, not estimated**. WA Health publishes
*Electronic prescribing and dispensing software approved in Western Australia*
(D00121.31, updated 23 December 2025), which names every approved product. Vendors seek WA
approval as a matter of course, so it is the best public census that exists. Products are
collapsed to vendors (Fred Classic + Plus = 1; Corum LOTS + Clear Dispense = 1; Genie +
Gentu + VIP.net = 1; MedicalDirector + Helix = 1).

| Segment | Total | Reachable | Line | Basis |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| Dispensing / pharmacy software | 9 | 6 | API | **Counted** — 11 approved products |
| Prescribing / EMR / clinic software | 21 | 10 | API | **Counted** — 27 approved products |
| Aged care eNRMC / medication charts | 8 | 5 | API | **Counted** — 8 approved products |
| Telehealth & digital health (not already listed) | 20 | 12 | API | Estimate |
| Medication management / adherence / clinical AI | 20 | 12 | API | Estimate |
| Hospital pharmacy systems | 8 | 2 | API | Estimate |
| Health funds / insurers | 8 | 1 | API | Estimate |
| Pharma market access teams (AU) | 50 | 22 | Market Intel | Estimate |
| Consultancies / HTA / market research | 25 | 14 | Market Intel | Estimate |
| **Total** | **169** | **84** | | |

Two things the verified count changed. The April draft **undercounted prescribing/EMR
vendors by half** (10 vs 21 actual) and **missed aged care medication-chart software
entirely** — 8 vendors who all need PBS data and are the least likely of anyone to have
in-house PBS expertise. It also overcounted dispensing vendors slightly (12 vs 9 actual).

The top-line barely moved — 84 reachable against a guessed 87 — which is mild evidence the
sizing method is sound even where the composition was wrong.

**Every paying customer is ~1.2% of the reachable market.** There is no funnel to optimise
and no growth loop to engineer. This is an account-by-account business, and the plan should
read like a sales plan, not a product plan.

### Two lines, different economics

- **Line A — API subscriptions.** Software vendors renting a maintained pipeline. Lower ACV,
  shorter cycle, sticky once embedded. Sellable today.
- **Line B — Market intelligence.** Pharma market access and consultancies buying the
  `/market/*` aggregates. Roughly 3× the ACV, longer cycle, annual prepay, and buyers who
  already spend six figures with IQVIA. **Blocked on validating the Tier C derivations** —
  today it would mean selling our least-defensible numbers to our most demanding buyers.

## 6. Pricing

Dollar amounts were never set. Setting them is now urgent, because §8 shows pricing power is
the single biggest lever in the model.

| Tier | Monthly | Annual | Anchor |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Base | Free | — | 1,000 req/mo. Evaluation, not a business line. |
| Core (T1) | $390 | $3,900 | Cheaper than one afternoon of engineer time per month. |
| Clinical (T2) | $1,190 | $11,900 | ~8% of one engineer's loaded cost. |
| Intelligence (T3) | $2,900 | $29,000 | Replaces a data engineering project. |
| Market (T4) | $3,500 | $42,000 | Annual commit only. Priced against IQVIA, not against us. |
| Enterprise | from $6,000 | from $72,000 | SLA, escrow, dedicated ingest, embargo access. |

**Anchor the conversation on their cost, not ours.** A senior AU backend engineer is a
~$175K loaded cost. A vendor spending even 8% of one engineer on PBS pipeline maintenance is
burning $14K/year to be *less* correct than we are. Clinical at $11,900 is not a data
subscription; it is a cheaper, better version of a job they are already paying for.

Do not discount below Core. A $99 tier attracts researchers and students who generate support
load and never expand.

## 7. Revenue model

Generated by `scripts/revenue_model.py`. It is funnel-driven — accounts worked, meeting rate,
pilot rate, close rate, pilot lag, churn — not a customer count picked to hit a target. It
also models **account recycling**: in a market of 84 you exhaust the list inside a year, and
survival depends on re-working the "no"s, whose pipelines drift and whose engineers leave.

### Base case — the correctness pitch lands with a minority

| | Revenue | Costs | Net | Exit MRR | Exit ARR | Customers |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Year 1 | 21,170 | 23,875 | (2,704) | 3,271 | 39,252 | 2.8 |
| Year 2 | 87,227 | 25,345 | 61,882 | 10,830 | 129,966 | 7.5 |
| Year 3 | 182,860 | 32,606 | 150,255 | 18,924 | 227,088 | 12.1 |

Monthly break-even month 6 · cumulative payback month 15 · **peak cash required $11,708**.

### All three scenarios

| | Bear | Base | Bull |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Year 3 exit ARR | 36,989 | 227,088 | 822,359 |
| Year 3 customers | 3.3 | 12.1 | 30.6 |
| Monthly break-even | month 29 | month 6 | month 3 |
| Cumulative payback | never | month 15 | month 6 |
| 3-year cumulative net | (27,163) | 209,433 | 1,024,448 |
| Peak cash required | 29,692 | 11,708 | 10,537 |

**Bear** is the market saying no: the correctness pitch doesn't land, vendors stay with what
they built. **Bull** requires one thing specifically — a recognisable anchor vendor who lets
you name them. That single reference is what separates $227K from $822K, because in a market
of 84 accounts everyone knows everyone.

### The number that should decide this

Three-year forgone salary at $175K is **$525,000**. Base case cumulative net is $209,433.
A senior AU engineer averages ~$152K base; at the 12% super guarantee plus payroll tax and
overheads, $175K loaded is conservative.

**On a pure cash basis, the base case loses to taking a job — by about $315K.** Only the bull
case beats employment. That is the actual decision, and no amount of endpoint-building
changes it.

This is not an argument against doing it. It is an argument for (a) being honest that the
base case is a well-paid side business rather than a salary replacement, (b) keeping the cash
requirement near $12K so a failure is cheap, and (c) treating "can I land one nameable anchor
customer" as the real question, since that is the entire gap between the two outcomes.

### Cost assumptions

Hosting $150→$450/mo · professional indemnity + public liability $260/mo (non-optional for
health-adjacent data) · accounting/ASIC $250/mo · tooling $90/mo · marketing $400→$1,400/mo ·
payment fees 2% · one-off legal $6,000 in month 1 for licensing advice, T&Cs and a contract
template.

## 8. Sensitivity — what to actually go and learn

Each assumption varied ±30%, measured on 36-month cumulative net (base case $209,433):

| Assumption | −30% | +30% | Swing |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **ARPA (pricing power)** | 123,803 | 295,062 | **171,260** |
| Contact → meeting rate | 125,311 | 292,280 | 166,969 |
| Meeting → pilot rate | 125,311 | 292,280 | 166,969 |
| Pilot → paid rate | 125,311 | 292,280 | 166,969 |
| Reachable market size | 151,995 | 244,746 | 92,751 |
| Recycle uplift | 163,412 | 255,453 | 92,041 |
| Accounts worked / month | 172,198 | 224,866 | 52,668 |
| Monthly churn | 221,918 | 197,715 | 24,202 |
| Inbound (year 3) | 199,080 | 219,785 | 20,706 |

Three conclusions, and they should drive everything in §9:

1. **Price is the biggest single lever — bigger than market size.** Charging 30% more (+$171K) beats
   finding 30% more customers (+$93K), and costs nothing. Do not underprice to reduce friction.
2. **Conversion beats volume.** Working *more* accounts is nearly the weakest lever
   (+$53K); converting them better is the strongest (+$167K). Ten deeply-researched
   conversations beat eighty cold emails, which is fortunate, because eighty don't exist.
3. **Churn and inbound barely matter.** Do not build a marketing site, a blog, or an SEO
   strategy in year one. It is the lowest-yield thing on the list.

## 9. How to take this further

Sequenced as gates with kill criteria, not a feature roadmap. Do not proceed to the next gate
until the current one clears.

### Gate 0 — This week. ~$100 and two emails. Blocks everything.

- **Register `pbsdata.io`.** It is unowned as of 2026-08-12. Someone reading this plan over
  your shoulder could take it.
- **Write to the Department of Health** asking, in writing, whether commercial
  redistribution of PBS API data is permitted and under what licence. The pbs.gov.au
  copyright page reserves all rights beyond personal reference use; the data.gov.au PBS
  datasets are CC BY 3.0 AU; the API's own terms are stated nowhere.

> **Kill criterion:** if the Department says commercial redistribution requires a licence we
> cannot get, the business does not exist in this form. Find out now, not after Stripe.

### Gate 1 — Weeks 1–6. Ten conversations. Write no code.

You have 99 endpoints and zero customers. The constraint is not product.

- Pick 10 named accounts from §5, weighted to telehealth and medication-management (they
  built most recently, most hastily, and have the least internal PBS expertise).
- Ask three questions: *Who maintains your PBS pipeline? What broke last time the schedule
  changed? How would you know if a patient cost was wrong?*
- Do **not** demo. Do not mention tiers. Listen for whether "we might be wrong and can't
  tell" is a felt pain or a shrug.

> **Kill criterion:** fewer than 3 of 10 express real discomfort about correctness → the
> wedge doesn't exist, and you should stop. Cost so far: ~$100 and three weeks.

### Gate 2 — Weeks 6–12. The audit wedge. First paid pilot.

This is the strongest go-to-market available, and it falls straight out of §3.

**Offer a free PBS pipeline audit.** Ask for a sample of their output — patient costs for
200 PBS codes, or their item/restriction join. Diff it against ours. Hand back a short report
naming the specific rows where they disagree with pbs.gov.au.

It works because it is not a pitch, it is a gift; it demonstrates the product instead of
describing it; it is impossible to argue with, since the referee is the government's own
data; and it is nearly free to deliver — the comparison harness already exists in
`scripts/validate_tier_a_joins.py` and `ingest/join_validation.py`.

Then: **deliver the first pilot by hand.** Manual invoice, a shared API key, your mobile
number. No Stripe, no self-serve, no dashboard.

> **Kill criterion:** no signed pilot by week 12 → revisit pricing (§8 says try *higher*
> first) or accept the bear case.

### Gate 3 — Months 3–6. Productionise, but only what a paying customer asked for.

- Deploy to Railway; `status.pbsdata.io`; uptime monitoring with real alerting
- Stripe, once you have 3+ customers and manual invoicing is genuinely annoying
- Public docs (FastAPI already generates them) and a one-page pricing page
- A written SLA you can actually meet — 99.5%, not 99.99%
- **Ship a nightly Postgres dump / Parquet export alongside the API.** This is more
  strategically important than it sounds: it kills the "we can't take a single-point-of-
  failure dependency on a one-person company" objection, which is otherwise fatal with
  exactly the vendors worth the most. They load it locally; you are a data supplier, not a
  runtime dependency. It also matches how the Department intends the data to be used.

### Gate 4 — Months 6–12. Earn the anchor.

The bull case is entirely about one nameable reference. Pursue it deliberately: pick the most
credible vendor from the dispensing or EMR segment, offer a discounted multi-year deal
explicitly in exchange for a public logo and a quote, and over-serve them.

Also address the solo-operator objection head-on before it is raised: **source escrow** (they
get the code if you disappear), the data dump above, and a documented bus-factor plan.

### Gate 5 — Months 9–18. Open Line B.

Before selling a single `/market/*` endpoint, **validate the Tier C derivations** — golden
cases for patient cost, safety net, substitution, 60-day pairs and the price-pressure index,
or honest labelling that they are our opinion rather than PBS fact. Selling unvalidated
heuristics to pharma market access teams is the fastest way to lose the reputation that §3
says is the entire asset.

Then sell Line B against IQVIA's price, not against Line A's.

### Gate 6 — Month 18+. Only if Gates 1–5 cleared.

- **Geographic replication.** The model transfers to PHARMAC (NZ) and NHS BSA dm+d (UK). The
  ingest/validate/serve architecture is the reusable asset; the schema is not.
- **Honest exit option.** A vendor in §5 buying the codebase and the pipeline outright is a
  legitimate and probably underrated outcome — likely worth more than three years of base-case
  cash, and worth keeping in view rather than discovering by accident.

## 10. Risks

| Risk | Severity | Response |
|---|---|---|
| **Redistribution not licensed** | **Fatal** | Gate 0. Resolve in writing before anything else. |
| Base case loses to a salary | High | Accept it explicitly, or aim at the anchor customer that makes it a business. Keep cash exposure near $12K. |
| Solo operator can't win serious vendors | High | Data dump + source escrow + documented bus factor (Gate 3/4). |
| Tier B/C numbers are wrong | High | Gate 5. Do not sell them until validated. Correctness is the whole brand. |
| Buyers already built it | Certain | This is the premise, not a risk. The pitch is that what they built is subtly wrong. |
| Government ships a real query API | Low | Stated design is bulk retrieval; a 1-req/20s shared limit is not an accident. |
| MIMS adds self-serve | Low–Med | Different product. They sell clinical content; we sell PBS operational correctness. |
| Ingest breaks on a schema change | Routine | Already mitigated — quality gates fail the schedule rather than serving bad data. |

## 11. The decision

The engineering is done and it is good. The market gap is real and permanent. The pitch
should be correctness, not access, and it improves with age.

But the base case is a $227K-ARR business in year three that pays less than a job, in a
market of 84 accounts, with an unresolved licensing question and an unregistered domain.

So the plan is not "finish the product." It is: **spend $100 and six weeks finding out
whether ten named humans feel the pain we think they feel** — and be willing to stop if they
don't. Everything after Gate 1 is contingent on Gate 1.

Run `python scripts/revenue_model.py` and argue with the assumptions. They are all wrong; the
question is which ones are wrong enough to matter.
